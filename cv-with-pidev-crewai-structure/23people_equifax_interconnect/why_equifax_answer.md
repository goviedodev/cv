# Why are you interested in working at Equifax?

**Gonzalo Oviedo Lambert** · Senior Developer Java / Spring Boot — InterConnect (via 23people)

---

## Written answer (application form / email)

Three reasons, in order of weight.

**First, I have spent my career on the consumer side of the decision Equifax makes.** At Citibank, Coopeuch and Banco Santander I built the banking backends that request a risk assessment and then act on the answer — approve, decline, price, escalate. I have implemented that downstream logic in Java and in Oracle PL/SQL, inside environments where correctness and auditability were non-negotiable. InterConnect is the other side of that same call: the platform where the rule is actually evaluated. Moving from consuming decisions to engineering the platform that produces them is the most natural next step my background offers, and it is one I genuinely want.

**Second, the problem shape matches what I am good at.** A decisioning platform is business rules, data integration and REST services under strict correctness and availability constraints — not a domain that rewards cleverness over traceability. That is the discipline I learned in fifteen years across banking, a healthcare management system and a global e-commerce platform: design for the case where being wrong is expensive. I want to keep working on systems where that constraint is real, and few products in Chile carry it more directly than the one behind a credit decision.

**Third, the Santiago Development Center is a genuine engineering hub, not a local branch.** I have worked daily in English with teams in the USA, Ukraine, India and LATAM, and that international scope is something I want to keep — but from Chile, where I am based. Equifax offers exactly that combination: global technical scope without leaving the country. That is rare here, and it is a large part of why this role stands out to me rather than another Java position.

I would add one thing. For the past year my day-to-day development has run through AI agent harnesses that I design and extend myself, not just consume. I am looking for a place where that leverage compounds — a hub that concentrates a meaningful share of global engineering is where it does. That is a reason I am interested in Equifax specifically, and not only in the role.

## Notes for delivery

- **Lead with the banking-to-bureau angle.** It is specific, true, and no generic candidate can say it. Avoid opening with "Equifax is a global leader in data and analytics" — that reads as researched, not motivated.
- **Do not overclaim credit-risk domain knowledge.** The honest claim is *adjacent* experience (consuming risk decisions, implementing rule logic in PL/SQL), not risk modelling. If asked directly, say so — it lands better than bluffing.
- **The AI-harness point is a closer, not an opener.** It answers "why you" more than "why us." Keep it last and short.
- **If asked "why not a bank again?"** — the honest answer is scope: at a bank you serve one institution's decisions; at Equifax the platform serves the market. That is a bigger engineering problem.
