# "Why are you interested in working at Equifax?"

**Role:** Senior Developer — My Equifax (D2C) · **Date:** 2026-08-24

> There is an earlier answer to this same question in `23people_equifax_interconnect/why_equifax_answer.md` (July 2026). **It is not reusable here.** That one was built around InterConnect — "going from consuming credit decisions to engineering the platform that produces them" — and My Equifax is the opposite end of the product line: consumer-facing, not the B2B decisioning engine. The angle below is retargeted.

---

## Written version (form / email) — 250 words

Three reasons, in order of weight.

**The side of the table.** For fifteen years I built software for institutions: banks at Citibank, Coopeuch and Santander, a hospital management system, a global manufacturer's e-commerce platform. In all of them the end user was someone the organisation held data about, and the software served the organisation. My Equifax inverts that — the product exists so the person can see and act on their own file. Building on that side, with the data set that actually shapes people's financial lives, is not a common opportunity in Chile.

**The problem shape is one I have solved before.** A consumer-facing platform sitting on financial data comes down to availability, correctness that holds under audit, and a data model that outlives whatever framework is on top of it. I have built that at smaller scale: a healthcare system clinical staff used every day, an ed-tech platform serving 1,800 schools and 500k daily requests, and a mobility platform running in production today. The stack in your posting — Java, Spring Boot, PostgreSQL, cloud — is what I used to build all three.

**The working model.** I have spent years in English-speaking distributed teams across the US, Ukraine and India, and I want to keep that international scope while staying based in Chile. A team here that operates fully in English is exactly that combination, and there are not many of them.

I will also be straightforward: this is the third Equifax role I have applied to this year. The interest is not new.

## Delivery notes

- **Do not open with "Equifax is a global leader in data and analytics."** It reads as researched, not motivated. Every other candidate opens that way.
- **Do not overclaim the credit-bureau domain.** The defensible position is adjacent experience — banking backends, regulated environments — not credit-risk modelling. Consistent with `respuesta_experiencia_equifax.md`.
- **Only say the first paragraph if you mean it.** It is a motivation answer, not a fact answer; a rationale you do not hold will not survive the follow-up question. If the consumer-side framing does not reflect what you actually want, cut it and lead with the second reason — the problem shape — which stands on its own.
- **The "third application" line is optional but strong.** It is verifiable, it preempts them noticing on their own, and persistence reads well. Drop it only if you would rather they not connect this to the August application via 23People.
- **⚠️ Verify before naming it:** the July answer referred to the "Santiago Development Center" as a genuine engineering hub. This posting does not mention it, and I did not confirm it still exists under that name. The version above says "a team here that operates fully in English" instead — safe against a wrong product/org name.
- **Figures used:** 1,800 schools / 500k daily requests only. No percentages, nothing self-reported.
- **Consistency:** if the same form also asks about your experience or education, the answers in this folder are written to sit alongside each other without repeating the same anecdote in each one.
